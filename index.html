Projections

Ranges are the 10th to 90th percentile of 4,000 simulated games. “Over” columns are the model's probability of beating that line, not a sportsbook price. Players need at least 3 earlier games to be projected.

Team ratings

Expected points added (EPA) per play compared with an average team, adjusted for opponents, home field and game situation. Higher is better for every column, defenses included.

Player efficiency ratings

Rating = percentile among qualified players at the position (100 = best). The bar shows the 80% range: a wide bar means the data can't pin this player down yet. Efficiency is opponent-adjusted EPA per dropback (QB), per designed carry (RB) or per target (WR/TE), shrunk toward the position average.

Each bar is the defense's rate relative to the league (0 = average), shrunk toward average and built only from earlier games. For the offense, longer bars to the right are good news: the defense allows more there.

All scores

Show every score for every season, market and model

CRPS and MAE: lower is better. Coverage: share of outcomes inside the 80% interval (target 80%). ECE: average calibration error of over/under probabilities; “noise limit” is the most a perfectly calibrated model would show at that sample size. Bias: average predicted minus actual.

How this works

  • Every market is built the same way: volume (targets, carries or pass attempts) → successes (catches, completions) → yards drawn from real NFL plays, plus touchdowns and interceptions per play. Each game is simulated 4,000 times.
  • B1 uses a player's own recent averages. B2 uses his share of the team's volume × the team's expected volume from the spread and total.
  • Redistribution re-scales shares when teammates are out so the team still adds up. Opponent adjustment scales efficiency by what the defense allowed to that position. Each is kept only if it improved the 2024 tuning season.
  • QB pass attempts include an “early exit” chance (injury or benching), fit on earlier seasons.

Honest notes

  • Rules for each phase were written and committed before its 2025 test season was scored, and 2025 was scored once. Changes made before scoring are listed as deviations in each phase's PREREGISTRATION.md.
  • Phase 1 (WR/TE): yards passed; receptions improved on the baseline, but not by enough to rule out chance.
  • Phase 3a (10 more markets): none passed. RB rushing yards and attempts beat the baseline by 3–5% but are slightly miscalibrated. Anytime TD improved a little, also slightly miscalibrated. For QB passing, no addition helped, so it stays on the baseline. The shared rushing setup made QB rushing yards worse.
  • Average predictions match reality in every market; the misses are in the shape of the ranges.
  • Watch item: in 2026 weeks 1–5, WR/TE yards ranges covered 75% instead of 80%.
  • Phase 3b (ratings): player ratings beat raw averages at every position on 2025. Team run ratings passed; team pass ratings were better on 2018–2024 but not clearly on 2025.
  • Phase 3c (matchups): run-gap, receiver-archetype and team-rating features survived development testing by tiny margins (under 0.2%), then made forecasts slightly worse on 2026 and 2025. Depth-of-target and pressure features did not survive. Projections therefore stay on the earlier locked models.
  • Projections for an unplayed week assume each team's actives from its last game. Check injury news before relying on any number.
  • Nothing here is a betting recommendation. The model has not been compared with sportsbook prices yet (Phase 2 is waiting on historical odds).
